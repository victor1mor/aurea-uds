03A

AppShell

11 patternsAppShell page
Floating

The frame of a whole application

Brand and global actions on the topbar, the sections on the sidebar, the screen in the middle. It is the only component that owns page landmarks — one per document, never nested — and it emits the skip link for free, which is what closes WCAG 2.4.1 without the consumer writing anything.

Open pattern
Flush

A topbar joined to the page

`topbarVariant="flush"` drops the floating surface and welds the bar to the top edge. Pick one of the two per application and hold it: floating on one screen and flush on the next reads as two products, not as two screens.

Open pattern
Collapsed

The sidebar out of the way

`sidebarCollapsed` is controlled by the consumer, like `open` on the palette: the shell never decides on its own that the navigation should disappear. The toggle in the brand slot is emitted by the shell and drives the same sidebar on small screens, where it opens as a popover instead of a column.

Open pattern
Sidebar to the top

The sidebar from top to bottom

`layout="sidebar"` drops the bar that spans the window: the sidebar runs from the top to the bottom, the brand lives at its top and the person at its bottom, and only the list scrolls. The collapse button sits beside the page title (`AppShell.SidebarToggle`).

Open pattern
Sidebar to the top, with a bar

A bar over the content alone

Pass `topbar` and the bar comes back over the content column only, with the collapse button first in it. What goes in `topbarActions` sits at its far end, away from the title — search, the theme switch, the account. The sidebar keeps running from top to bottom.

Open pattern
Capsule

The sidebar that becomes a capsule

`sidebarCollapsible="capsule"`: collapsed, the floating sidebar keeps its rail of icons with the control radius — a capsule — instead of the card radius. The button at the seam collapses and expands it, and so does Ctrl+B (Cmd+B on a Mac).

Open pattern
Off-canvas

The sidebar that leaves the screen

`sidebarCollapsible="offcanvas"`: collapsed, the sidebar leaves the screen and the content takes the whole width. The button beside the title brings it back. It is the reference's off-canvas mode.

Open pattern
Button inside

The collapse button inside the sidebar

`sidebarToggle="none"` keeps the shell from placing its own button, and the app puts `Sidebar.Toggle` where it wants — here, as the `action` of the brand at the top of the sidebar. Collapsed, the button moves under the logo.

Open pattern
Sections, docked

Sections with the list beside them

`sidebarPanel="docked"`: the first level of `navItems` becomes sections — a rail of icons — and the list of the chosen section sits in a panel beside it. Collapsing hides the panel and keeps the rail.

Open pattern
Sections, overlay

Sections with the list over the page

`sidebarPanel="overlay"`: the rail of sections stays narrow, and the list of a section opens over the page, next to its icon. Focus moves into the list, and Escape or a click outside closes it.

Open pattern
Current item

The current item of the sidebar, five ways

`sidebarIndicator` sets how the item you are on is drawn: `brand` (the default), `segment` (the capsule of the selected tab, with no line), `segment-dot`, `segment-line` and `subtle`. Switch them here, on the real shell.

Open pattern

Open pattern

Open pattern

Amber, not the Aurea yellow. Until August 2026 the warning token resolved to the brand colour in dark theme — same pixels, two different meanings — so warning was not a tone at all. Open pattern

Open pattern
Tone

Open pattern
Group

Stacked actions

A vertical group for a menu of actions on a card or in a side panel. The buttons all measure the same width — without that the column comes out ragged, which is the one defect a vertical group cannot have. It stays role="group" and announces no orientation: it does not navigate by arrow, and promising keyboard that does not exist is worse than staying quiet.

Open pattern
Responsive

The same button in three container widths

One button, one value, one viewport — three container widths. The step comes from the space the component was placed in, not from the size of the window, so a sidebar and a main column can show the same component at different steps at the same time. This is the proof that separates the two mechanisms: if the three measured the same, what existed would be a media query under another name. The parent needs `container-scope`, which names the container — an unnamed one matches the nearest ancestor that happens to be a container, including one somebody else declared for another reason.

Open pattern
Responsive

Reacting to the window instead

The other mechanism, for when the decision really does belong to the application and not to the slot: the step follows the viewport. Same axis, same scale, and the prefix in the emitted class says which of the two is measuring — `md` is always 768px, and never means two things depending on where you read it. Resize the window to see it move; the example above will not move, because its containers have fixed widths.

Open pattern
Responsive

Every family follows its own scale

The same three containers, now with one piece from each family. Control, mark and avatar come from the control height and step together; the icon inside the button comes from the glyph scale and does NOT follow the button — a large button with a huge glyph is not a larger button, it is a different drawing. The two behaviours in one picture are the point: sharing a step is measured, never assumed.
