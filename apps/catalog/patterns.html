Patterns

223 compositions over 80 components. A pattern is a component already assembled one specific way — Component → Variant → Composition. You copy a pattern; you configure a component.

Accordion 2

Questions on a marketing pageFAQ

The title is the question in the reader's own words, not a category. An accordion earns its place when the reader wants one answer, not all of them — otherwise plain headings read faster.

Advanced options, folded awaySettings

The defaults are enough for most people, so the rest starts closed. Folding is not hiding: the summary line says what is inside, so nobody has to open it to find out.

Alert 6

Inline noticeInfo

Context that belongs to the section it sits in — an Alert stays put; a Toast would vanish.

Failure with retryDanger

What broke, plus the way out in the same block. An error without an action is a dead end.

Degraded serviceBanner

Page-width notice for something that affects the whole surface, dismissible by the reader.

Nothing foundEmpty

The empty result of a filter: says why it is empty and offers the way back.

Determinate uploadProgress

Known progress with a label. Unknown progress is a Skeleton or a spinner, never a fake bar.

Loading placeholderProgress

The shape of the content before the content — Skeleton is aria-hidden by design.

AppShell 3

The frame of a whole applicationFloating

Brand and global actions on the topbar, the sections on the sidebar, the screen in the middle. It is the only component that owns page landmarks — one per document, never nested — and it emits the skip link for free, which is what closes WCAG 2.4.1 without the consumer writing anything.

A topbar joined to the pageFlush

`topbarVariant="flush"` drops the floating surface and welds the bar to the top edge. Pick one of the two per application and hold it: floating on one screen and flush on the next reads as two products, not as two screens.

The sidebar out of the wayCollapsed

`sidebarCollapsed` is controlled by the consumer, like `open` on the palette: the shell never decides on its own that the navigation should disappear. The toggle in the brand slot is emitted by the shell and drives the same sidebar on small screens, where it opens as a popover instead of a column.

AspectRatio 3

A media slot that reserves its spaceDefault

The box holds the shape before the image arrives, so nothing below it jumps when it does. That is the whole job: not cropping the picture, but stopping the layout from shifting under the reader — the failure a card grid makes most visibly.

One ratio held across a gridDefault

Cards of different image heights read as a broken grid even when every card is correct. Fixing the ratio in one place is what makes the row line up, and it survives a source image nobody checked.

A square, for identityDefault

`ratio={1}` is the default because the square is the shape identity uses — an avatar, a logo, a QR code. Naming it explicitly in the code is still worth it: a reader who sees `ratio={1}` knows the square was chosen, not inherited.

Avatar 1

Initials when there is no picturePerson

The fallback is not a placeholder, it is the normal case: most people in most systems have no photo. Initials come from the name so two rows never look alike by accident.

AvatarGroup 1

Who is on this runStack

Overlapping faces plus a count for the rest. The cutoff exists because a row of twelve avatars stops being information and becomes texture — the number carries what the faces no longer can.

Badge 6

Version tagPrimary

Short metadata next to a title — the release the page documents.

Maturity scaleSuccess

The whole maturity ladder in one row, so a reader places an item at a glance.

Count on a labelNeutral

A number that changes, kept quiet: neutral, so it never competes with a status.

Badge next to StatusNeutral

Badge classifies, Status reports a condition. Together they answer what and how.

Categories, not statusesCategory

Eight colour names mark a group — a section, a kind of item — and say nothing about health, so they never compete with success or danger. Yellow is left out: it belongs to the brand. The word and the icon carry the meaning; the colour only helps the eye find the group again.

One category in three emphasesCategory

The categories take the same three emphases as every other badge: soft for a list, solid for the one that must stand out, outline (since 0.34.0 a light tint, no line) when the surface is already busy.

Banner 2

Something that affects the whole pageNotice

A banner spans the region it is about; an alert sits inside it. Dismissible only when the message is genuinely optional — a banner about degraded service that can be closed is a banner nobody sees twice.

The cookie noticeConsent

variant="consent" asks before setting cookies that are not necessary: reject them, accept all, or choose by category in a window. Rejecting is as easy to see as accepting — same weight, same place —, the optional categories start off, and after the choice the confirmation takes the focus. Put the notice at the top of the page, before the skip link, and do not fix it to the screen. To change the choice later, show it again from a link in the footer, with value set to what you stored. On this static page the buttons do not answer; in your app they do.

Breadcrumb 1

Where this page sitsPath

Every level is a link except the last, which is the page itself and carries aria-current. A breadcrumb whose last item is also a link tells the screen reader that the user can navigate to where they already are.

Button 24

With leading iconPrimary

The main action of a screen, with the glyph repeating the verb — never replacing it.

SubmittingPrimary

Busy state for a form submit: spinner, aria-busy and the label kept for context.

Confirm and cancelPrimary

The pair that closes a dialog: one primary, one ghost. Cancel is never a button of equal weight.

With textOutline

Secondary action that still needs an edge — a neutral choice next to a primary one.

With trailing chevronOutline

Opens a menu or a list. The chevron announces that something unfolds.

Destructive with iconDanger

Irreversible action. The red plus the glyph make the consequence readable before the click.

Toolbar trioGhost

Three low-emphasis actions side by side, where the surface below must keep reading.

Icon onlyGhost

Square target with an accessible name — an icon without a label needs one.

Segmented rangeGroup

Coordinated set of the same control. Each button stays tabbable — that is what separates it from a Toolbar.

With KbdPrimary

The action and the key that fires it, together — shown and announced by aria-keyshortcuts.

Destructive with KbdDanger

Irreversible plus a shortcut: the key is spelled out so nobody discovers it by accident.

Inline actionLink

Inside a sentence, where a capsule would break the line: text, underlined at rest.

Pinned filterToggle

A filter that stays on: aria-pressed keeps the state visible and audible after the click.

Full width on a narrow screenPrimary

Form footer on a phone: the action takes the whole width so the thumb cannot miss it.

With counterGroup

Action carrying a live number: the Badge rides inside the label, not beside it.

The two axesTone

Appearance is how much weight the box has; tone is what the colour means. The old names are shortcuts for pairs — variant="danger-outline" and appearance/tone below render the same button.

Confirming a safe outcomeTone

Success tone for an action that lands somewhere good — publishing, approving, marking done. Filled, because tone and weight are independent: choosing green does not force the button to be quieter.

Warning is not the brandTone

Amber, not the Aurea yellow. Until August 2026 the warning token resolved to the brand colour in dark theme — same pixels, two different meanings — so warning was not a tone at all. It has its own identity now, picked by measuring perceptual distance from both neighbours: the brand on one side, the destructive red on the other.

Informational action in running textTone

Info tone on the link appearance: a pointer to context that is neither a warning nor a commitment.

The same tone across appearancesTone

One tone, four weights — and every cell exists. A tone never loses its filled form: each one owns a colour and the text colour that goes on top of it, chosen per theme and verified for AA contrast, exactly as the destructive tone always had.

Stacked actionsGroup

A vertical group for a menu of actions on a card or in a side panel. The buttons all measure the same width — without that the column comes out ragged, which is the one defect a vertical group cannot have. It stays role="group" and announces no orientation: it does not navigate by arrow, and promising keyboard that does not exist is worse than staying quiet.

The same button in three container widthsResponsive

One button, one value, one viewport — three container widths. The step comes from the space the component was placed in, not from the size of the window, so a sidebar and a main column can show the same component at different steps at the same time. This is the proof that separates the two mechanisms: if the three measured the same, what existed would be a media query under another name. The parent needs `container-scope`, which names the container — an unnamed one matches the nearest ancestor that happens to be a container, including one somebody else declared for another reason.

Reacting to the window insteadResponsive

The other mechanism, for when the decision really does belong to the application and not to the slot: the step follows the viewport. Same axis, same scale, and the prefix in the emitted class says which of the two is measuring — `md` is always 768px, and never means two things depending on where you read it. Resize the window to see it move; the example above will not move, because its containers have fixed widths.

Every family follows its own scaleResponsive

The same three containers, now with one piece from each family. Control, mark and avatar come from the control height and step together; the icon inside the button comes from the glyph scale and does NOT follow the button — a large button with a huge glyph is not a larger button, it is a different drawing. The two behaviours in one picture are the point: sharing a step is measured, never assumed.

ButtonGroup 4

Time rangeRange

Day, week, month over the same chart — the label names the set, the buttons the spans.

Pinned filtersMultiple selection

Many-of-many: each button is its own toggle, so two can be on at once.

View switchSingle selection

One-of-many is not this component: SegmentedControl carries one value and one tab stop.

Share actionsIcons

Glyph-only set at the end of a card: one name for the group, one for each action.

Calendar 3

Date pickerPicker

There is no DatePicker component: a picker is a trigger plus a popover plus this calendar. Three parts you already have, and the trigger is what names the field.

Booking with days closedPicker

The reason this component exists. A native date input cannot say "not before Thursday, and never a Sunday" — everything else about a plain date, it does better.

When the native input winsNative

One plain date, no closed days: the native picker is smaller, knows every locale, and is the one the person already knows how to use on their phone.

Card 10

Titled surfaceBase

The plain floating surface: title, one line of context, radius 22.

Header with actionBase

Title on the left, the action on the right, on the same baseline. The card owns its own header.

Selectable tileInteractive

A card that is a target. The interactive variant carries the hover and the focus ring — and it requires `render`, because only the caller knows whether the tile is an action (<button>) or a link (<a href>).

KPI rowMetrics

Three numbers of the same weight. One line, equal columns, trend beside the value.

Member rowIdentity

Avatar, name and role — the identity line that repeats in every list of people.

Facts listDetail

Term and value in two columns — for a record's attributes, not for tabular data.

Activity timelineDetail

What happened, in order, with the time on the side. History, not a feed of live events.

A summary at the top of an articleBase

Three short points before the long text, for the reader who has thirty seconds. It is a card with a short title and a list, not an alert: an alert interrupts, a summary waits to be read. The check icon is decoration; the list is announced as a list.

The card that stands outContrast

The main card of a screen. In the light theme it is the dark theme's card; in the dark theme, the regular card (the yellow outline went with the lines in 0.34.0). Inside it the whole dark theme applies, so text, badges and buttons adjust by themselves. Not for a list of choices: in the dark theme its outline is close to `selected`.

A colour stroke on topAccent

A stroke on the top edge in the tone's fill colour, the same in both themes. It is decoration: the title already says the subject.

Chart 3

One series over timeFrame

Chart is the frame, not the drawing: it carries the accessible name, the sizing and the surface, and the series comes in as a child of the chart library. That split is what lets the drawing engine be swapped without touching the page.

Two series, read togetherFrame

Two bars side by side answer a question one bar cannot: not how many runs, but how many of them failed. The legend is required here — with two series the colours stop being decoration and start carrying meaning.

The whole reading surfaceFrame

Grid, axis, legend and tooltip on one chart — the assembled form, so the pieces are seen doing their jobs together instead of one at a time. `defaultIndex` pins the tooltip because a static page has no pointer; in an application it is not needed.

ChartLegend 2

Naming the colours of a stacked seriesBottom

Stacking hides which band is which, so the legend stops being optional: it is the only place the reader learns that the darker band is the failures. It sits under the drawing by default, where it does not compete with the axis for the first read.

Legend above the drawingTop

`verticalAlign="top"` reads the names before the shapes, which is what a dashboard tile wants when the drawing is short and the reader is scanning several tiles at once. Everything else stays: the component still takes the series from the engine, never a hand-written list.

ChartTooltip 2

The exact value under the pointerSingle series

An area chart shows a shape, not a number; the tooltip is where the number lives. It reuses the same `.tooltip` surface as the rest of the system, so a value read on a chart looks like a value read anywhere else.

Every series at one point in timeMulti series

With more than one series the tooltip becomes a small table: the label is the point on the axis, and each row is a series with its colour swatch. This is the comparison the drawing cannot make, because two bars of different heights do not tell you their difference.

Checkbox 2

One box that gates the actionConsent

The label is the whole sentence and it is clickable — a checkbox whose label is not part of the target is a 16px hit area, which fails on touch. Never pre-checked when it grants permission.

A set where several answers are rightList

Checkbox, not radio, is the difference between 'pick any' and 'pick one'. The rule is the answer, not the look: if zero selected is a legitimate state, it is a checkbox.

Cluster 3

A row of actions that wraps instead of overflowingDefault

Buttons side by side with the gap of the system, and a wrap that happens before anything is pushed off screen. Writing `display:flex` by hand gets the row right and the wrap wrong, which is only visible on the narrow viewport nobody tested.

Tags under a headingDefault

A cluster of badges is the shape a filter list and a tag list share. The primitive is what keeps the vertical rhythm right when the second row appears — the case a hand-written flex row almost always gets wrong.

Metadata beside a titleDefault

Title, status and time on one line, aligned on their centres rather than their boxes. The alignment is the point: three elements of different heights on a hand-written row sit on three different baselines.

CodeBlock 3

The install lineCopyable

The shortest useful block: one command, one button. `copyable` is what makes it a block instead of a paragraph — a reader who has to select the text by hand will select the prompt with it.

A snippet under a headingCopyable

The shape every documentation page repeats: a sentence that says what the code does, then the code. The block keeps its own horizontal scroll, so a long line never widens the page around it.

Output, not sourcePlain

`language="text"` is the default for a reason: not everything in a block is code. A stack trace or a command's output is quoted verbatim, and pretending it is a language would colour tokens that are not tokens.

CodeEditor 1

A snippet the reader can changeEditable

An editor, not a code block: the reader types. Use it where the change is the point — a query, a config, a rule — and use CodeBlock everywhere else, because a read-only editor costs a bundle it does not earn.

Collapsible 4

Advanced options, folded awayUncontrolled

The default answer for a form section most people never open. `defaultOpen` is left off and the state stays inside the component — a consumer that does not need to read the state should not have to hold it.

A detail already unfoldedOpen

`defaultOpen` starts unfolded but leaves the reader in charge of folding it back. Use it when the content is the point of the screen and the fold is a courtesy, not a filter.

Fold driven from outsideControlled

`open` plus `onOpenChange` when something else in the screen decides — a "expand all" button, a deep link, a search that has to reveal the match it found. Passing `open` without `onOpenChange` freezes it, which is a legitimate read-only state and not a bug.

A section that cannot be opened yetDisabled

`disabled` keeps the row in place while the content is unavailable, instead of removing it. A section that disappears and comes back renumbers everything around it; a section that stays says the same thing without moving anything.

Combobox 2

Picking one out of manySingle

A select stops working somewhere around twenty options — the eye cannot scan them. A combobox lets the user type what they already know instead of hunting for it.

Options that belong to sectionsGrouped

A flat list of forty time zones is a wall; the same forty under continents is a map. Pass groups — `{label, items}` — in the same `items` prop, and the engine's filter runs inside each section, dropping a section that has nothing left to show. `empty` writes what appears when nothing matches, which is where a list stops saying No results and starts saying what the user can do about it.

CommandPaletteShell 3

Every command on one keystrokeOpen

The palette is the keyboard route to anything the application can do. The field takes focus on open and the list narrows as you type, so the whole interaction is one gesture — never a menu you have to find first.

Results narrowed by what was typedFiltered

Filtering is the consumer's job, not the shell's: it holds `query` and decides what matches. Keeping it outside is what lets the same palette search components, files or an API — the shell never assumes the shape of what it is searching.

Nothing matchedEmpty

The one state a palette must not skip. A list that simply vanishes reads as a broken palette; EmptyState says the search ran and found nothing, which is a different fact and the one the reader needs.

ContextMenu 1

Right-click on an itemCanvas

The menu the mouse expects on an item, and the keyboard reaches with Shift+F10. It never replaces a visible control: everything here has another way in.

DataGrid 1

A list you act on in bulkSelectable

Selection turns a table into a work surface: pick the rows, then act once. The header checkbox selects the page, never the whole dataset — a promise a static table cannot keep.

DataList 4

The facts about one recordDefault

A definition list is the honest markup for "this thing has these properties". Every value here belongs to the same run; nothing on the list compares it to another run.

Values that are componentsDefault

`value` takes any node, so a status reads as a status and an identity reads as an identity. The alternative — spelling "Succeeded" as text — loses the one thing the reader scans for when the list is long.

Inside a card, under a headingDefault

The shape a detail panel repeats: a title that names the record and the list that describes it. The card supplies the surface; the list supplies the structure — neither tries to do the other's job.

A spec sheet in groupsDefault

A long list of facts reads better cut by subject. Each group is a card with its own title and its own list; the grid lays the cards side by side and stacks them on a phone. No group component to learn — the parts already exist.

DependencyGraph 1

A network map, top to bottomNetwork

The internet on top and each layer below. The kind of device is a word with an icon beside it; badges carry the count and the status; ports name the ends of the lines; the line's drawing says what kind of link it is without colour — double for fibre — and its state comes apart, as a colour, a word and a cross when down. The legend builds itself in two parts: the kind of link and the state. Repeated links between the same two devices collapse into one with their count.

Dialog 2

Destructive confirmationConfirm

The pause before something irreversible. The title asks the question, the body says what is lost, and the confirming button carries the danger tone — never the cancel.

Short form in a dialogForm

A dialog is the right home for a form only when it is short enough to fit without scrolling. The primary action repeats the verb of the title, so the user never has to re-read what they are confirming.

Drawer 2

Detail panel beside the listRight

The row stays on screen and the detail slides in beside it — the reason to pick a drawer over a dialog is exactly this: the context does not disappear.

Filters that stay open while you readLeft

Filters on the left, results on the right. A dialog would cover the results the filters are changing, which is the one thing a filter panel must not do.

DropdownMenu 3

The overflow menu of a table rowRow actions

Three dots at the end of the row. Destructive items sit last, after a separator, so the pointer never lands on Delete while reaching for Rename.

A primary action with variants behind itSplit action

The button does the common thing; the chevron opens the rest. The label of the button is the action itself, never the word More.

A menu that holds STATE, not just commandsView menu

The View menu of any application: things that toggle, things that are mutually exclusive, and a section label that names the group instead of pretending to be an item. Until 28/08/2026 the Aurea menu could express none of these — `items` had a single shape, and the engine underneath had five (G-API-02).

EmptyState 2

Nothing here yet, and one way forwardFirst run

The empty state of a new account is not an error: it says what will appear and offers the one action that fills it. Never the same copy as a search with no results — those are different situations wearing the same layout.

The filter matched nothingNo results

Tabs 3
